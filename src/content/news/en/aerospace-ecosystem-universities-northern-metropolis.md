The Northern Metropolis is Hong Kong's defining urban strategy for the next 20 years. Within it, **three university towns** — San Tin, Hung Shui Kiu and Ta Kwu Ling — are the key infrastructure for nurturing aerospace talent.

## Three towns, three roles

**San Tin University Town**

Located in the Loop area, Building 1 of the Hong Kong Park is scheduled for completion by end-2026 and will be the flagship. The town brings together top local, mainland and international universities and institutions — including research teams in aerospace, AI and life sciences.

**Hung Shui Kiu University Town**

Located in the core of the Northern Metropolis, applications from post-secondary institutions are being invited this year for campus development. The town follows the "One Town, Five Elements" layout — education, technology, industry, talent and urban development.

**Ta Kwu Ling University Town**

Located near the Northern Metropolis border with Shenzhen, it forms a "one-hour research circle" with mainland GBA cities.

Together the three towns span over **1,000 hectares** — comparable to world-leading university towns.

## What this means for aerospace talent

**First, the research runway gets closer.** University towns concentrate local universities' aerospace departments, research labs and industry bases in one urban area — no more commuting between campus, lab and industrial park.

**Second, industry-academia integration deepens.** The "Five Elements" layout is, in essence, a micro innovation system. Students can join real aerospace industry projects during their studies.

**Third, international collaboration gets easier.** The Loop's "one zone, two parks" arrangement enables seamless cooperation between Hong Kong and Shenzhen research bodies. Future international aerospace institutions will likely choose university towns as their landing pad.

## Timeline

- **End of 2026**: Hong Kong Park Building 1 (Loop) completes
- **2027–2028**: Hung Shui Kiu University Town campus construction begins
- **2030s**: All three towns fully operational

This means **today's undergraduates will hit peak aerospace-industry hiring around 2030.**
