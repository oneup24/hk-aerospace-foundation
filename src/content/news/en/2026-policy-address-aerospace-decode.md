In September 2026, the Chief Executive delivered the third Policy Address of the current term. For the first time, the "frontier technology" chapter explicitly names aerospace and introduces four measures that directly affect the sector — an unusual level of detail in past addresses.

Here are the four tailwinds most relevant to Hong Kong aerospace's future.

## Tailwind 1: The Frontier Technology Task Group

The Innovation, Technology and Industry Bureau sets up a new Frontier Technology Task Group covering AI, new energy, new materials, **aerospace**, quantum technology and more.

**Why it matters:** For the first time, Hong Kong places aerospace at the same strategic tier as AI and quantum at the government-architecture level. Aerospace is no longer just "tech" — it is a key industry that needs its own strategic playbook.

## Tailwind 2: The Aerospace Technology Special Call

A new round of the Aerospace Technology Special Call has been launched, with emphasis on supporting research aligned with national space missions.

**Why it matters:** This is the most direct funding line for local universities, startups and research institutions pursuing space-related research. Hong Kong aerospace projects now have a dedicated express lane for funding.

## Tailwind 3: HKEX Listing Rule 18C

HKEX will consider enhancements to Chapter 18C of the Listing Rules to attract emerging-industry enterprises such as aviation and aerospace to raise capital in the city.

**Why it matters:** Local aerospace startups get a real exit path. Hong Kong is positioned to become a top listing venue for aerospace companies, and the venture ecosystem around the sector will deepen.

## Tailwind 4: PhD Seats and University Towns

- UGC-funded research postgraduate places to **9,600** by 2030/31 (+30%)
- Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme: **400 → 550** places by 2029/30 (+37.5%)
- Three Northern Metropolis university towns spanning **1,000+ hectares**

**Why it matters:** Aerospace talent is the start of the education → research → industry chain. Without PhDs, research seats and university towns expanding in parallel, the best policies have no one to absorb them. Today, that chain is being systematically reinforced.

## Our reading

These four tailwinds send one common signal: **Hong Kong's aerospace policy window is wide open.**

But let's be honest — policy is the stage. Talent is the lead. How we expose more local young people to space, and how we turn curiosity into careers, is the defining question of the next decade.
