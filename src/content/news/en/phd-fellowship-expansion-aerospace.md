Among the many numbers in the 2026 Policy Address, one deserves the close attention of every young Hong Konger eyeing aerospace: **the Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme (HKPFS) will grow from 400 to 550 places — a 37.5% increase.**

This is more than a number. For local students who want a career in aerospace research, it is a ticket.

## Why the PhD Fellowship?

In Hong Kong, the PhD Fellowship is the "first capital" of a research career. It provides a monthly stipend of around HK$28,100 (2026/27 standard), but more importantly, it admits you into a top local research group — including in aerospace, AI, quantum and new energy.

In other words, **winning the Fellowship means stepping into the core of Hong Kong's aerospace research community.**

Until now, that ticket was capped at 400. From the 2029/30 academic year, it grows to 550. The extra 150 places are Hong Kong's incremental source of future aerospace talent.

## Why now?

Three forces are converging:

1. **National mission expansion** — crewed spaceflight, lunar exploration, satellite internet and other national projects need top research talent.
2. **Local research funding rising** — the Aerospace Technology Special Call and other dedicated funding lines give PhD students more research opportunities.
3. **Local aerospace programmes growing** — HKUST, PolyU, CityU and others already offer aerospace or adjacent programmes; the research runway for PhDs is widening.

The meeting point of these three forces is the best window in years for Hong Kong PhDs to enter aerospace research.

## How to apply

Local students targeting entry from 2027/28 onwards should start with:

- **Academic record** — a master's or honours bachelor's degree, ideally in an aerospace-adjacent field
- **Research proposal** — a clear research interest aligned with local or national space missions
- **References** — from academic supervisors or industry experts
- **Language** — strong Chinese and English; mainland or international exposure is a plus
