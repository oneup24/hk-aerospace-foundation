Hong Kong's aerospace story is being rewritten.

For the past decade, Hong Kong's role in China's aerospace landscape has been that of a research contributor, a talent exporter, and an international window. Standing at 2026, we see something deeper, more systematic, and more structural — Hong Kong is being pushed to the centre of the aerospace stage by both national strategy and local policy at the same time.

This is not a slogan. Look at three numbers:

- **9,600** — UGC-funded research postgraduate places will grow to 9,600 by academic year 2030/31, a roughly 30% expansion.
- **+37.5%** — The Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme will rise from 400 to 550 places by 2029/30.
- **1,000+ hectares** — The three Northern Metropolis university towns together span over 1,000 hectares in planned area.

These three numbers point to one thing: the next decade will give research talent — especially in aerospace, AI, quantum and new energy — an unprecedented runway in Hong Kong.

## The next decade will lack prepared talent

The opportunity window is open. The Frontier Technology Task Group has been set up. A new round of the Aerospace Technology Special Call is launching. Three university towns are moving in parallel. Hong Kong's aerospace policy toolbox is the fullest it has been in a decade.

But opportunity does not become outcome on its own.

The real question: when the aerospace stage is built, who can stand on it? Who can hold it up?

That is why we founded HATDF. We believe — **talent is the true launchpad of Hong Kong aerospace.**

## Four tailwinds we see

First, **national strategy** — Aerospace is designated a priority frontier technology; national missions will keep expanding demand for top talent.

Second, **local policy** — The 2026 Policy Address places aerospace in the new Frontier Technology Task Group and launches the Aerospace Technology Special Call.

Third, **education capacity** — PhD seats, research seats and university towns are expanding in parallel — more local pathways into aerospace.

Fourth, **international interface** — Under "One Country, Two Systems", Hong Kong is the natural bridge to international aerospace networks; 5 local universities already offer aerospace or adjacent programmes; InnoHK has attracted world-class labs.

## What we do

Our four directions — scholarships, youth exchange, research funding and public outreach — exist to translate these four tailwinds into real people.

Hong Kong's aerospace window is open. The next decade belongs to prepared talent.
