The Hong Kong aerospace story can be told as a number: **the distance from 0 to 1.**

In the past, Hong Kong was a bystander to national space missions — local scientists contributed to some research through academic exchange, but there was no direct pipeline in industry, talent or policy.

Today, Hong Kong is a core node in the national aerospace map — the Policy Address places aerospace among frontier technologies, and three currents — policy, capital and talent — are converging at the same moment.

That 0-to-1 shift is the new chapter.

## Three stages of Hong Kong aerospace

**Stage 1 (1990s–2010s): Research participation**

Local scientists collaborated with mainland research bodies on parts of national space missions. Hong Kong's role was peripheral and complementary.

**Stage 2 (2010s–2020s): Industry germination**

Local universities began offering aerospace-related programmes and research centres. HKUST, PolyU and CityU trained the first generation of locally grown aerospace talent. InnoHK attracted several space-relevant labs.

**Stage 3 (from 2026): Core node**

The 2026 Policy Address is a watershed: for the first time, government-level policy designates aerospace a frontier technology, launches a dedicated Aerospace Technology Special Call, and enhances HKEX Chapter 18C to welcome aerospace listings. Hong Kong enters the core of the national aerospace map.

## Why 2026?

Three currents converge:

1. **National strategy** — The 15th Five-Year Plan designates aerospace a priority frontier technology; national missions (crewed lunar landing, satellite internet, deep-space exploration) keep expanding.
2. **Local policy** — The Policy Address places aerospace in the Frontier Technology Task Group, with funding, seats and land as matched measures.
3. **Local foundation** — 8 UGC-funded universities, 5 with aerospace or adjacent programmes, a multi-year track record of InnoHK, and an existing local startup ecosystem.

These three currents developed separately for a decade — and met for the first time in 2026.

## Who writes the next chapter?

Over the next decade, who writes Hong Kong's aerospace story?

**Students** — those who walk into the university towns and pick aerospace.

**Researchers** — local scientists who win the PhD Fellowship and step into frontier labs, anchoring Hong Kong as a real node of national aerospace research.

**Founders** — entrepreneurs who use HKEX Chapter 18C to build a commercial aerospace layer in Hong Kong.

**Educators and nonprofits** — those who bring space closer to grassroots youth and expand the talent base.

Their common thread: **they are being nurtured, supported, seen.**

That is why we — the Hong Kong Aerospace Talent Development Foundation — exist.

The new chapter has begun. We are here, walking alongside every author.
